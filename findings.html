Findings


What Could Data-Driven Matching Contribute, and What Can It Not Solve?

POTENTIAL CONTRIBUTIONS

  • Consumer discovery: Preference-based ranking can reduce search and comparison costs.
  • Information priorities: Gap scores show which product fields should be improved first.
  • Trust support: Certification, traceability, testing evidence, and explanations can provide clearer decision signals.

CHALLENGES, RISKS & SAFEGUARDS

  • Incomplete data: Products with less documentation may be ranked too low even when their underlying quality is strong.
  • Popularity concentration: Recommendation systems may repeatedly favor already visible or information-rich products.
  • Digital participation: Producers and consumers with lower digital access may benefit less.

LIMITATIONS

  • Sampling: The 300 respondents were not selected through probability sampling and may not represent all Hangzhou consumers.
  • Stated intentions: Willingness to buy or use a tool does not equal actual purchasing behavior.
  • Product coverage: 30 products cannot represent all producers, categories, or sales channels in Chun’an.

FINAL CONCLUSION

Data can make rural products easier to understand and match, but inclusive market access still depends on trustworthy evidence, logistics, product quality, producer participation, and accountable system design.
